Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) is evaluated against a remarkably uniform control group: the base model, matched supervised fine-tuning, sometimes rejection-sampling SFT. All are learning arms whose accuracy grows with the demonstration budget. We pre-registered, before the second task was run, a decision rule comparing GRPO against the maximum over a four-arm control group, one arm of which is a deterministic symbolic solver that does not learn. Across two program-annotated financial QA tasks and three backbones spanning 14x, all 17 frozen-test effect sizes are negative, from -1.06 to -16.42 points; judged against the warm start alone the same runs report +15.07. The mechanism is a moving baseline: flat in the demonstration budget, the solver attains the maximum at 0.5B and 1B; at 7B continued SFT replaces it.

This extended version audits that instrument and reports a boundary we found ourselves. In our favour, a cross-fitted estimator that selects the control arm on one half of the test set and scores it on the other is negative in 17 of 17 groups, so the max does not manufacture the result. Against us, only 2 of 17 groups survive Holm correction, no GRPO run completed one epoch over its prompt pool, and re-running the smallest-margin 7B cell at ten times the prompt coverage reverses it decisively (Delta = +6.74 and +6.38 across two training seeds), clearing our own Go threshold; at the registered budget nothing changes. The 7B configuration therefore marks a budget boundary rather than a capability one, and the quantitative claim rests on the 11 groups at 0.5B and 1B. Pre-registration, deviation logs, per-item outcome vectors and the code that produced them are released.
